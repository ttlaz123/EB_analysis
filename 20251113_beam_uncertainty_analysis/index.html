For a specific map frequency \(\nu\), we consider a corrected beam \(B^{\nu}_{\text{corr}}(\ell)\) that is a slightly shifted version of the original beam \(B^{\nu}_{\text{orig}}(\ell)\):

\[B^{\nu}_{\text{corr}}(\ell) = B^{\nu}_{\text{orig}}(\ell(1+\epsilon))\]

Here, \(\epsilon\) ranges from \(-0.05\) to \(0.05\), representing a beam error shift of up to 5 percent. The shift is in the \(\ell\) direction, meaning the shift in \(B(\ell)\) could be greater or lesser depending on \(\ell\) and \(B(\ell)\).

Effect on Power Spectra

This beam broadening translates to a scaling effect on the power spectra bandpowers. We define the scaling factor for a map at frequency \(\nu\) as the ratio of the corrected to the original beam:

\[ \text{scaling}(\nu) = \frac{B^{\nu}_{\text{corr}}(\ell)}{B^{\nu}_{\text{orig}}(\ell)} = \frac{B^{\nu}_{\text{orig}}(\ell(1+\epsilon))}{B^{\nu}_{\text{orig}}(\ell)} \]

This factor simulates the effect if the power spectra were originally multiplied by the original beam. Therefore, to obtain the corrected power spectrum, we must multiply the original spectrum by this scaling factor.

For a cross-map \(\nu_1 \times \nu_2\):

If the original power spectrum, \(D^{\nu_1 \times \nu_2}_{\text{orig}(\ell)}\), was incorrectly calibrated due to the beam uncertainty, the new corrected power spectrum becomes:

\[ \text{supfac}^{\nu_1 \times \nu_2}(\ell) = A_\ell \times B_\ell^{\nu_1} B_\ell^{\nu_2} \]

If the original power spectrum, \(D_{\ell, \text{orig}}^{\nu_1 \times \nu_2}\), was incorrectly calibrated due to the beam uncertainty, the new "corrected" power spectrum becomes:

\[ D^{\nu_1 \times \nu_2}_{\text{corr}}(\ell) = D^{\nu_1 \times \nu_2}_{\text{orig}}(\ell) \times \left( \frac{B^{\nu_1}_{\text{orig}}(\ell(1+\epsilon))}{B^{\nu_1}_{\text{orig}}(\ell)} \right) \times \left( \frac{B^{\nu_2}_{\text{orig}}(\ell(1+\epsilon))}{B^{\nu_2}_{\text{orig}}(\ell)} \right) \]

The corrected power spectrum is obtained by applying the beam scaling factor for each frequency component, \(\nu_1\) and \(\nu_2\), of the cross-spectrum map.
